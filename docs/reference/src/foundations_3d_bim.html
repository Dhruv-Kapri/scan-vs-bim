3D Reality Capture & BIM — Foundations

The domain, not the project: how 3D measurement, point-cloud learning and building information fit together — plus things to read and follow casually.

General-fluency primer · complements (does not repeat) the project reading list · Oct 2026 · every link verified against primary sources

What this is. The project reading list covers the specific pipeline papers (registration, IFC tooling, BCF, LOA, carbon databases). This one is for the field itself: the conceptual spine of 3D geometry, measurement and building information. Nothing here duplicates that list. §1–3 are the ideas worth genuinely internalising; §4–6 are the modern landscape; §7–8 are for relaxed, ongoing absorption.
If you only do three things: (1) read Solà's micro Lie theory (§1) and Lague's M3C2 (§3), so you think in poses with uncertainty and in significant, not raw, differences; (2) skim Volk et al. on BIM for existing buildings (§5) and the RICS whole-life carbon standard (§6) to speak the field's language; (3) follow one or two idle sources from §8 (start with Stachniss on YouTube) so the field stays in your peripheral vision.

1 — Why 3D Is Different (geometry you can't hand-wave)

A micro Lie theory for state estimation in robotics — Solà, Deray & Atchuthan (2018), arXiv The clearest intuitive route into SO(3)/SE(3), exp/log maps and perturbations on manifolds, which is what every registration optimiser is secretly doing. arxiv.org/abs/1812.01537
Lie Groups for 2D and 3D Transformations — Ethan Eade (rev. 2017), self-published note Short, derivation-first companion to Solà; the reference you keep open while implementing pose updates. ethaneade.com/lie.pdf
Visualizing quaternions — Grant Sanderson & Ben Eater (2018), interactive explorable Builds the 4D intuition for unit quaternions and why they avoid gimbal lock, without any algebra up front. eater.net/quaternions
On the Continuity of Rotation Representations in Neural Networks — Zhou, Barnes, Lu, Yang & Li (2019), CVPR Why quaternions and Euler angles are bad regression targets and 6D representations are good: geometry meeting deep learning. arxiv.org/abs/1812.07035
Associating Uncertainty With Three-Dimensional Poses for Use in Estimation Problems — Barfoot & Furgale (2014), IEEE T-RO 30(3) paywalled How to put a covariance on a rotation and a pose, and propagate it. Registration without error bars is only half an answer. doi.org/10.1109/TRO.2014.2298059

2 — Point-Cloud Processing Ideas (what you do before any model sees the data)

Surface reconstruction from unorganized points — Hoppe, DeRose, Duchamp, McDonald & Stuetzle (1992), ACM SIGGRAPH Computer Graphics 26(2) paywalled The origin of PCA-based normal estimation and consistent orientation, the quiet workhorse behind most point-cloud geometry. doi.org/10.1145/142920.134011
Multidimensional binary search trees used for associative searching — Jon Louis Bentley (1975), Communications of the ACM 18(9) paywalled The k-d tree paper. Every neighbourhood query, normal and ICP correspondence step leans on this structure. doi.org/10.1145/361002.361007
Semantic point cloud interpretation based on optimal neighborhoods, relevant features and efficient classifiers — Weinmann, Jutzi, Hinz & Mallet (2015), ISPRS J. Photogrammetry & Remote Sensing 105 paywalled Shows that the choice of neighbourhood size and shape changes everything downstream; the classic argument for multi-scale thinking. doi.org/10.1016/j.isprsjprs.2015.01.016
Graduated Non-Convexity for Robust Spatial Perception — Yang, Antonante, Tzoumas & Carlone (2020), IEEE Robotics and Automation Letters 5(2) A clean unifying view of robust estimation (outliers, M-estimators, why RANSAC is one answer among several) with a practical alternative. arxiv.org/abs/1909.08605
MAGSAC++, a fast, reliable and accurate robust estimator — Barath, Noskova, Ivashechkin & Matas (2020), CVPR What modern RANSAC descendants look like: no hand-tuned inlier threshold. Useful for seeing how the idea has matured. arxiv.org/abs/1912.05909
Past, Present, and Future of Simultaneous Localization And Mapping — Cadena et al. (2016), IEEE T-RO 32(6) The field's own map of itself: front-end versus back-end, outliers, loop closure, and what robust perception should mean. arxiv.org/abs/1606.05830

3 — Measurement & Metrology Thinking (what a number from a scanner actually means)

Evaluation of measurement data: Guide to the expression of uncertainty in measurement (GUM) — JCGM 100:2008, BIPM The international standard vocabulary and method for uncertainty budgets and error propagation. Dense but definitive. bipm.org/documents/20126/2071204/JCGM_100_2008_E.pdf
Guidelines for Evaluating and Expressing the Uncertainty of NIST Measurement Results (TN 1297) — Taylor & Kuyatt (1994), NIST A short, readable distillation of the GUM; the quickest way to get type A/B uncertainty and combined standard uncertainty into your head. physics.nist.gov/Pubs/guidelines/TN1297/tn1297s.pdf
Statistics notes: Absence of evidence is not evidence of absence — Altman & Bland (1995), BMJ 311 Two pages on the logic behind "not observed does not mean not there". Reread it every time a scan has a hole where a wall should be. doi.org/10.1136/bmj.311.7003.485
Accurate 3D comparison of complex topography with terrestrial laser scanner (M3C2) — Lague, Brodu & Leroux (2013), ISPRS J. Photogrammetry & Remote Sensing 82 paywalled Cloud-to-cloud comparison with a per-point confidence interval, so you can say whether a change is significant. The template for honest deviation maps. doi.org/10.1016/j.isprsjprs.2013.04.009

4 — Deep Learning on 3D (from PointNet to foundation models)

Deep Learning for 3D Point Clouds: A Survey — Guo, Wang, Bennamoun, Lu, Huang & Wan (2021), IEEE TPAMI 43(12) The standard survey of classification, detection and segmentation architectures up to 2020; read it for the taxonomy. arxiv.org/abs/1912.12033
PointNet: Deep Learning on Point Sets for 3D Classification and Segmentation — Qi, Su, Mo & Guibas (2017), CVPR The founding idea: respect permutation invariance and consume raw points directly. arxiv.org/abs/1612.00593
PointNet++: Deep Hierarchical Feature Learning on Point Sets in a Metric Space — Qi, Yi, Su & Guibas (2017), NeurIPS Adds local neighbourhoods and hierarchy, the pattern (sample, group, aggregate) that most later models inherit. arxiv.org/abs/1706.02413
Dynamic Graph CNN for Learning on Point Clouds — Wang, Sun, Liu, Sarma, Bronstein & Solomon (2019), ACM Trans. Graphics 38(5) EdgeConv on a feature-space k-NN graph: point clouds as dynamic graphs. arxiv.org/abs/1801.07829
KPConv: Flexible and Deformable Convolution for Point Clouds — Thomas, Qi, Deschaud, Marcotegui, Goulette & Guibas (2019), ICCV A true convolution with kernel points in continuous space; strong on outdoor and building-scale segmentation. arxiv.org/abs/1904.08889
Point Transformer — Zhao, Jiang, Jia, Torr & Koltun (2021), ICCV Vector self-attention over local neighbourhoods; the start of the transformer era for point clouds. arxiv.org/abs/2012.09164
Point Transformer V2: Grouped Vector Attention and Partition-based Pooling — Wu, Lao, Jiang, Liu & Zhao (2022), NeurIPS Fixes the efficiency and expressiveness issues of v1; the middle step of the series. arxiv.org/abs/2210.05666
Point Transformer V3: Simpler, Faster, Stronger — Wu et al. (2024), CVPR Trades exact neighbour search for serialised point order and scales up cleanly. The current default backbone. arxiv.org/abs/2312.10035
Pointcept — Pointcept contributors, GitHub The codebase behind PTv1-v3 and Sonata, with unified benchmarks on ScanNet, S3DIS and others. Read the configs to see how the field trains. github.com/Pointcept/Pointcept
VGGT: Visual Geometry Grounded Transformer — Wang, Chen, Karaev, Vedaldi, Rupprecht & Novotny (2025), CVPR Feed-forward prediction of cameras, depth and point maps from images: the learned replacement for classical structure-from-motion. arxiv.org/abs/2503.11651
Geometric Deep Learning: Grids, Groups, Graphs, Geodesics, and Gauges — Bronstein, Bruna, Cohen & Veličković (2021), arXiv The symmetry-first framing (equivariance to SE(3), permutation) that explains why 3D architectures look the way they do. arxiv.org/abs/2104.13478

5 — BIM, Digital Twins & Information Modelling (the field behind the file format)

Building Information Modeling: Why? What? How? — Borrmann, König, Koch & Beetz, eds. (2018), Ch. 1, Springer, open access A compact conceptual introduction to BIM, openBIM and model-based collaboration, written for engineers. doi.org/10.1007/978-3-319-92862-3_1
Building Information Modeling (BIM) for existing buildings: Literature review and future needs — Volk, Stengel & Schultmann (2014), Automation in Construction 38 paywalled Why as-built BIM is hard: the canonical statement of the problem this whole project lives in. doi.org/10.1016/j.autcon.2013.10.023
Semantic web technologies in AEC industry: A literature overview — Pauwels, Zhang & Lee (2017), Automation in Construction 73 paywalled Why IFC alone is not enough and what linked data adds; the information-science side of BIM. doi.org/10.1016/j.autcon.2016.10.003
BIMForum Level of Development (LOD) Specification — BIMForum (2025), BIMForum The industry reference for how much a model element is allowed to be trusted at each stage. Free, CC-licensed. bimforum.org/LOD/
Digital Twin: Mitigating Unpredictable, Undesirable Emergent Behavior in Complex Systems — Grieves & Vickers (2016), Transdisciplinary Perspectives on Complex Systems, Springer paywalled The widely cited origin of the term. Read it to see how much of today's usage stretches the original meaning. doi.org/10.1007/978-3-319-38756-7_4
Digital twins — Michael Batty (2018), Environment and Planning B 45(5) paywalled A short, sceptical editorial on what a digital twin is and is not: a useful counterweight to vendor definitions. doi.org/10.1177/2399808318796416

6 — Sustainability of the Built Environment (carbon, materials and reuse, conceptually)

Biogenic carbon in buildings: a critical overview of LCA methods — Hoxha et al. (2020), Buildings and Cities 1(1) A clear tour of why timber and bio-based carbon accounting is contentious: a good lesson in method choices driving results. doi.org/10.5334/bc.46
Concept for a BIM-based Material Passport for buildings — Honic, Kovacic & Rechberger (2019), IOP Conf. Series: Earth and Environmental Science 225 What a material passport is and how it would hang off a BIM model; the bridge between this project and reuse. doi.org/10.1088/1755-1315/225/1/012073
Whole life carbon assessment for the built environment, 2nd edition — RICS (2023), RICS professional standard The professional standard that defines how whole-life carbon is scoped and reported, in force since July 2024. rics.org/profession-standards/rics-standards-and-guidance/sector-standards/building-surveying-standards/whole-life-carbon-assessment-2nd-edition
Embodied Carbon Primer — LETI (2020), London Energy Transformation Initiative A practitioner-friendly primer on embodied carbon across life-cycle stages, with design levers. Free. leti.uk/ecp
Global Status Report for Buildings and Construction 2024/2025 — UNEP & GlobalABC (2025), UNEP The macro view: where buildings sit in global emissions and what policy is doing about it. unep.org/resources/report/global-status-report-buildings-and-construction-20242025

7 — Books & Longer Reads (mostly free / open)

State Estimation for Robotics (2nd ed.) — Timothy Barfoot (2024), Cambridge University Press; free PDF from the author Rigid-body kinematics, Lie groups, batch least squares and uncertainty, written for the exact maths this project uses. asrl.utias.utoronto.ca/~tdb/bib/barfoot_ser24.pdf
Computer Vision: Algorithms and Applications (2nd ed.) — Richard Szeliski (2022), Springer; free PDF via form The broad 3D vision reference: cameras, alignment, SfM, depth, neural rendering. szeliski.org/Book/
Modern Robotics: Mechanics, Planning, and Control — Lynch & Park (2017), Cambridge University Press; free preprint Chapters 2-3 are the gentlest rigorous treatment of rotations and SE(3) available,. hades.mech.northwestern.edu/index.php/Modern_Robotics
Multiple View Geometry in Computer Vision (2nd ed.) — Hartley & Zisserman (2004), Cambridge University Press paywalled The classic on projective geometry and estimation; worth owning for the chapters on estimation and error analysis. robots.ox.ac.uk/~vgg/hzbook/
Airborne and Terrestrial Laser Scanning — Vosselman & Maas, eds. (2010), Whittles Publishing paywalled The standard text on how laser scanners work and how their data is processed; ISPRS Karl Kraus Medal winner. whittlespublishing.com/Airborne_and_Terrestrial_Laser_Scanning
BIM Handbook: A Guide to Building Information Modeling (3rd ed.) — Sacks, Eastman, Lee & Teicholz (2018), Wiley paywalled The field's standard handbook: what BIM is, who uses it and how workflows change. wiley.com/en-us/BIM+Handbook (ISBN 978-1-119-28757-5)
Sustainable Materials: With Both Eyes Open — Allwood, Cullen et al. (2012), UIT Cambridge; free online Material efficiency as a climate lever: why using less steel and cement beats making it greener. Free chapters. withbotheyesopen.com
